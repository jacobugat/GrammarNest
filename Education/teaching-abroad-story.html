Chapter 1 · A new beginning

A suitcase, a map, and a new job

Sofia is twenty-four and lives in Colombia. She studies English every day and dreams of teaching abroad. One evening, she sees a job post from a small school in Busan, South Korea, and she fills out the application form. Two weeks later, Ms. Park, the school manager, asks her to come over for an online interview. Sofia is nervous, but she answers honestly: “I want my students to use English in real life.” She gets the job. Her mother gives her a notebook and says, “Write about your days.” Sofia needs to pack up her room, get a visa, and say goodbye to her family.

Six weeks later, Sofia arrives at Gimhae Airport with one suitcase and a lot of questions. She cannot find out which bus to take — the signs are in Korean and English. A woman at the information desk helps her: “Take bus 307 and get off near Haeundae Station.” Sofia writes the words in her notebook and smiles. She can do this.

Chapter 2 · The first lesson

“Hello, I’m Sofia.”

On Tuesday, Sofia walks into Room 204. Twelve students are inside — some are talking, some are looking at their phones, one is sleeping. Sofia sets up her name cards and says, “Good morning, I’m Sofia, your new English teacher.” She writes three questions on the board and asks students to stand up, find a partner, and talk. At first, nobody moves.

Sofia remembers Ms. Park’s advice: “Do not give up when a class is quiet.” She shows the activity with one volunteer, and soon everyone is talking and laughing. During the break, a student named Min-jun says, “My English is very bad.” Sofia answers, “Your English is growing. Just keep on practicing.” At the end of class, she asks students to hand in a small paper with one question about English. On the bus home, she reads: “How can I speak without fear?” She understands that question very well.

YOUR CHOICEMin-jun says, “I am afraid to speak.” Which response is most helpful?

Chapter 3 · Small steps

More than a teacher

By Friday, Sofia knows her way around the neighborhood. Ms. Park asks her to look after the school’s conversation club on Saturday, where students can hang out and practice English. Sofia comes up with an idea: a “city guide” activity, where students show a visitor their favorite place in Busan.

Saturday is rainy, and Sofia worries nobody will come. But slowly, students turn up — first Min-jun, then eight more. They talk about beaches, markets, and cafés, and Sofia does not correct every small mistake. “Great job,” she tells Min-jun. “You kept going.”

That evening, Sofia calls her mother. “I miss home,” she says, “but I am okay.” She looks at the map above her desk. She still does not know every street or every word. But she is starting to look forward to Monday — and to a whole year ahead.
